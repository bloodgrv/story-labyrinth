# Editor MultiView Guide

Split the chapter editor into resizable panes, keep several chapters (or the Lorebook, Outline, and other tool panels) open in tabs, and pop any of them out into a movable window when you want it visible alongside everything else.

> [!NOTE]
> MultiView lives entirely inside the **Editor** tool. A single pane with a single tab — exactly how the editor has always looked — is still the default; splitting and tabs are opt-in, not a new mode you have to switch into.

---

## 1. Splitting the Editor

Every pane has a small toolbar above its content:

| Button | What it does |
|--------|---------------|
| **Split right** (⧉) | Divides the pane into two side-by-side columns |
| **Split down** (⊟) | Divides the pane into two stacked rows |
| **✕** (Close pane) | Closes the whole pane — only shown once there's more than one |

A new split starts as a copy of whatever was showing in the pane you split — so splitting while looking at Chapter 3 gives you Chapter 3 in both halves, ready for you to scroll one side ahead or change its tab to something else entirely. Splits can be nested (split a pane, then split one of the results the other direction) to build an arbitrary grid.

Drag the thin divider between two panes to resize them — it highlights as you hover so it's easy to find, and turns solid while you're actively dragging it.

> [!TIP]
> Click anywhere inside a pane (or one of its tabs) to make it "active" — a small colored line appears along its top edge. New tabs, splits, and keyboard shortcuts all apply to whichever pane is currently active.

## 2. Tabs

Each pane has its own row of tabs, independent of every other pane. A tab isn't limited to a chapter — click the **+** button on a pane's toolbar to add:

- **Chapter** — opens a submenu listing every chapter in the story
- **Lorebook** — the full Lorebook browser, embedded right in the pane
- **Tags**, **Outline**, **POV**, **Notes**, **Beats** — the same panels available from the sidebar icons, scoped to whichever chapter is currently focused

This is the main reason to use MultiView even without splitting: keep the Lorebook open in one tab and your chapter in another, in the *same* pane, and switch between them with a click instead of hunting through the sidebar.

Hover a tab to reveal two small icons:

| Icon | Action |
|------|--------|
| ↗ | Float this tab (see below) |
| ✕ | Close this tab |

Closing a pane's last tab closes the pane itself; closing the very last tab in the very last pane isn't allowed — there always has to be something open.

## 3. Docking and Floating

Every tab can also become a **floating panel** — a movable, resizable window that sits on top of the editor instead of taking up space in the grid.

- Click the ↗ icon on a tab to **float** it. It detaches into its own window with a title bar.
- Drag the title bar to move it; drag the small corner mark in the bottom-right to resize it.
- Click **Dock** (⇱) in its title bar to send it back into the currently active pane as a tab, or **✕** to close it entirely.

Floating is useful for something you want visible no matter what else you're doing — a character's Codex state while you write their dialogue, for instance — without it eating into your split layout.

## 4. Keyboard Shortcuts

All MultiView shortcuts use **Ctrl+Alt** (**⌘+Option** on Mac) plus one more key, so they never collide with the app's existing shortcuts. They work even while your cursor is in the editor.

| Shortcut | Action |
|----------|--------|
| `Ctrl+Alt+→` | Split the active pane right |
| `Ctrl+Alt+↓` | Split the active pane down |
| `Ctrl+Alt+W` | Close the active pane |
| `Ctrl+Alt+J` | Next tab in the active pane |
| `Ctrl+Alt+K` | Previous tab in the active pane |
| `Ctrl+Alt+L` | Toggle a Lorebook tab in the active pane |
| `Ctrl+Alt+T` | Toggle a Tags tab (current chapter) |
| `Ctrl+Alt+O` | Toggle an Outline tab (current chapter) |
| `Ctrl+Alt+P` | Toggle a POV tab (current chapter) |
| `Ctrl+Alt+N` | Toggle a Notes tab (current chapter) |
| `Ctrl+Alt+B` | Toggle a Beats tab (current chapter) |

> [!TIP]
> The panel-toggle shortcuts (L/T/O/P/N/B) are smart about state: press one to open that panel as a tab, press it again to close that same tab. No need to hunt for the right tab to close it.

## 5. Your Layout Is Saved

Splits, tabs, and floating panels are all remembered automatically per story — close the app, come back tomorrow, and your panes come back exactly as you left them, including a floating panel's exact position and size. Nothing to save manually.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> If a saved tab pointed at a chapter that's since been deleted, that tab will say so plainly instead of getting stuck loading — just close it.

## 6. Troubleshooting

| Problem | What's happening |
|---------|-------------------|
| A shortcut doesn't seem to fire | Make sure you're on the Editor tool with a chapter open — MultiView shortcuts only run while the split editor itself is on screen |
| My split layout looks different than last time | Layouts are saved per story in your browser — using a different browser or clearing site data starts that story fresh |
| A floating panel disappeared off-screen | Click **Dock** isn't available if you can't see the panel — reopen the Guide's story from Chapters, then re-add the tab and float it again to reset its position |
| Closing a pane closed more than I expected | Closing a pane closes every tab inside it — check a pane's tabs before closing if it has more than one open |

---

MultiView layouts persist in your browser's local storage, the same way your current chapter and open tool are remembered — no account or server round-trip involved, and it works fully offline.
