# Brainstorm Guide

An intake and orientation hub for a story, not a structure desk or a lore factory. Brainstorm is where you talk through a premise before it's ready for the Outline, World-Building, or Notes — a chat-based space for exploring ideas, then handing the good ones off to the desk that actually owns them.

> [!NOTE]
> Brainstorm never writes directly into your Outline, Lorebook, or chapter prose. Everything it produces lands as a proposal you explicitly Accept or Open — see §3 below.

---

## 1. Starting a Brainstorm chat

_Path: **Brainstorm** in the left sidebar_

Opening the tool auto-selects your most recent Brainstorm chat, or creates one if this is your first visit. Use **New Chat** (in the Chats column) for a fresh session — e.g. one chat per major idea you're exploring, so you can find it again later.

> [!TIP]
> If a story has no chapters or lorebook entries yet, its card on the Stories page offers a **Start in Brainstorm** shortcut — the fastest way into a brand-new project.

## 2. Guided setup

The composer header offers a **Guided setup** button plus a style dropdown, for when you want a structured interview instead of a blank "so what's your story about?":

| Style | What it asks for |
| --- | --- |
| **Light** | Quick — just the essentials of your idea |
| **Standard** | Walks through premise, genre, characters, and setting |
| **Grill-me** | A full guided interview — premise, genre, protagonist, setting, and central conflict, pushed until they're solid |

Clicking **Guided setup** seeds the composer with an opening line matching your chosen style — it doesn't send automatically, and it isn't a scripted question-by-question wizard; the depth comes from how the model is instructed for that style, not a tracked state machine. You're always free to just type and ignore Guided setup entirely.

A **Setup checklist** in the Approvals panel (see below) tracks five fixed slots — Premise/Logline, Genre & Tone, Protagonist(s), Setting, Central Conflict & Stakes — as known or unknown. You can toggle them manually, and accepting a matching overview proposal (§3) flips one automatically.

## 3. Proposals: overview and handoffs

Brainstorm never writes directly into your project. It proposes, through two kinds of card that persist into the **Approvals** panel (inbox icon on the icon rail) the moment they appear — they don't disappear if you don't act right away:

| Kind | What it is | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Overview proposal** | A synopsis rewrite, a new Note, or a Project Memory entry | **Accept** performs the write |
| **Handoff** | A packet destined for Outline, World-Building, Notes, or Research | **Open** navigates to that desk with the content pre-seeded into its composer |

Both kinds also get a **Mark done** button — this is the only action that actually clears an item from the Active queue. Accept/Open perform the real write or navigation but deliberately leave the card in Active, so you don't lose track of something you meant to follow up on. A **Done** tab on the same panel shows everything you've marked complete.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A handoff to World-Building seeds a new Lorebook entry draft; handoffs to Outline/Research seed that chat's composer with the detail text. Nothing is created automatically — you still review and send in the destination desk.

## 4. Context & memory

The **Context** panel (sliders icon) controls what Brainstorm can see: Notes, Outline, Project Memory, Lorebook, and Chapter Summaries — all opt-in toggles, all off by default. Lorebook defaults off here specifically, unlike every other chat type, since Brainstorm is meant for pre-lore idea exploration rather than working against an established world.

## 5. Troubleshooting

| Problem | What's happening |
| --- | --- |
| A proposal I accepted is still showing in Active | By design — Accept/Open perform the write but don't clear the queue. Click **Mark done** once you're satisfied |
| Guided setup didn't ask me anything specific | It's prompt-driven, not a scripted interview — it seeds an opening line in your chosen style's register, then the conversation proceeds like any other chat |
| A handoff didn't create anything in the destination | Handoffs only *seed* the destination's composer or draft — you still have to review and send/save it there |
| The Lorebook toggle is off and I don't remember turning it off | It starts off by default for Brainstorm specifically (every other chat type defaults it on) |

---

Brainstorm chats, checklist items, and slot statuses all save with your story like any other data.
