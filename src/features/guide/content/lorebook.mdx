## Understanding Lorebook Levels

Story Labyrinth uses a hierarchical lorebook system with three levels, allowing you to share world-building across multiple stories or keep it specific to individual projects.

| Level | Scope | Use For |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Global** | All series and stories | Universal world-building, magic system rules, world geography, writing style references |
| **Series** | All stories in a series | Recurring characters, key locations, series timeline, shared lore |
| **Story** | Single story only | Story-exclusive characters, unique items, specific events |

> [!TIP]
> **How Inheritance Works:** When working on a story, you have access to all three levels: story-specific entries, series entries (if part of a series), and global entries. The AI automatically uses context from all accessible levels when generating content.

## What is a Lorebook?

A Lorebook is a powerful organisational tool that helps you keep track of all the important elements in your story's world. It serves as a central repository for characters, locations, items, events, and other story elements.

The Lorebook in Story Labyrinth is designed to not only help you organise your story elements but also to integrate them seamlessly with the AI writing process.

### Key Benefits

- Organise all your story elements in one place
- Maintain consistency throughout your story
- Automatically provide context to the AI
- Track relationships between story elements
- Filter and search for specific information

### Tag-Based System

The Lorebook uses a tag-based system to connect your writing with your Lorebook entries. When you write text that contains tags matching your Lorebook entries, those entries are automatically identified and can be used to provide context to the AI.

*For example, if you have a character named "Harry Potter" and you mention "Harry" in your text, the system will recognise and match this entry.*

## Entry Categories

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Characters** | People, beings, or entities with agency in your story |
| **Locations** | Places, settings, or environments where your story takes place |
| **Items** | Objects, artifacts, or possessions of significance |
| **Events** | Occurrences, happenings, or incidents in your story |
| **Notes** | General information, ideas, or concepts |
| **Synopsis** | Summary or overview of your story or chapters |
| **Starting Scenario** | Initial situation or setup for your story |
| **Timeline** | Chronological sequence of events or history |

> [!TIP]
> Open a story, then click "Lorebook" in the workspace sidebar. There's no separate series-level lorebook screen — series and global entries just appear inside every story's own Lorebook, alongside its story-level entries, each labeled with its level badge.

## Creating Lorebook Entries

Creating detailed Lorebook entries is essential for building a rich story world and providing the AI with the context it needs to generate appropriate content.

### Step 1: Access the Lorebook

Open a story and go to its Lorebook tool — you'll have access to global, series (if the story belongs to one), and story-level entries all from that one screen. To create at series level, set Level to "Series" in the Advanced panel (below); it'll then be visible from every story in that series, not just the one you created it from.

### Step 2: Create a New Entry

Click the "New Entry" button in the top-right corner of the Lorebook page. This opens the full entry editor directly — a wide panel with your entry form on the left and a docked World-Building chat on the right (desktop only), the same editor you get when opening any existing entry.

### Step 3: Write the Lore Sheet

The entry's primary surface is the **Lore Sheet** — a free-form markdown document (`sheetBody`) organized into headed sections, one per category (Character/Location/Item/etc. each get their own section set — e.g. a Character sheet has Core Identity, Physical Appearance, Wardrobe, Personality & Temperament, Wounds, Items, Background & Lifestyle, Character Motivations, and an optional **Sexuality** section). Above **Name** and an optional image, you'll find:

| Control | What it does |
|---------|---------------|
| **Section outline** | A sticky list of the sheet's own headings — click one to jump straight to it |
| **Insert template** | Fills in the category's standard section headings if they're missing |
| **Improve with AI** | One-shot AI tidy pass over your current sheet text — result lands in the (unsaved) field, Update/Create is still the real save |
| **Rework in chat** | Highlight a span inside one section and send it to the docked WB chat for a targeted rewrite — Accept splices just that span back in |

A pre-existing entry with no sheet yet gets one automatically reverse-compiled from its own description/Codex/place fields the first time you open it — never a blank template, so nothing you'd already written gets orphaned.

### Step 4: Sync structured fields (Character & Location only)

For **Character** and **Location** entries, a **Sync structured fields** button extracts the sheet's Wardrobe/Appearance/Wounds/Items (Character) or Landmarks/Place Details (Location) sections back into the entry's structured Codex state — never silently; it lands as a pending proposal in the same Codex approval tray as any chat-suggested change, showing an **added/removed** diff for list fields and a **before → after** diff for labeled fields, so you can see exactly what would change before approving. Every category's narrative sections also compile back into the entry's plain `description` field this same way. Syncing can also offer side proposals to other desks depending on category — a location's "Layout Notes" section as a Maps layout-brief card, an event/timeline entry's When/Summary as a single pending Timeline pin, or a Notes-category entry as a one-shot "create a note" offer.

For **Timeline** and **Event** entries specifically, there's a second button next to Sync — **Extract pins**. Where Sync only ever produces one pin per entry, Extract pins reads the whole sheet and proposes a separate pending pin for *every* beat it can identify — useful for a dense summary that compresses several days/scenes into one section (e.g. "Day 1: ... Day 2: ... Day 8: ..."). All extracted pins land marked **Planned** by default and wait in the Timeline tool's Pending tab for review — see the Story Timeline guide.

### Secrets (Character only)

Inside a character's **Advanced → Track Character State** panel, alongside Wardrobe/Wounds/Items, there's a **Secrets** box for facts you want tracked but *not* surfaced into AI-generated prose or chat context until you're ready — a spy's real identity, a hidden motive, anything meant to stay concealed until a reveal. Each secret has its own toggle:

- **Hidden / Revealed** — click the eye icon to flip it. This is always the authoritative switch: a hidden secret never enters AI context anywhere, a revealed one is treated like any other established fact.
- **Reveal at chapter…** — optionally pick a chapter. Once the Editor chat (or the RAG Scanner) is working on that chapter or later, the secret counts as revealed there automatically, without you having to remember to flip the toggle. It's purely additive — it never hides something you've already manually revealed.

You can also review and reveal secrets across every character in the story at once from the **Secrets** button in the Lorebook toolbar — see "The Secrets tab" in Managing Your Lorebook.

### Step 5: Advanced (level, category, tags, and other machine fields)

Click **Advanced** to access the entry's structured/organizational fields, collapsed by default so the Lore Sheet stays the primary thing you see:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Level & Series/Story** | Choose the scope: Global (everywhere), Series (shared within series), or Story (specific to one story) |
| **Category** | character, location, item, event, note, synopsis, starting scenario, or timeline — changing this reseeds an untouched auto-generated sheet to the new category's template |
| **Importance** | Set to major, minor, or background |
| **Tags** | Additional tags separated by commas — matched against your chapter text alongside the entry's own name |
| **Description** | The raw, structured description field — normally kept in sync by the Lore Sheet's own narrative sections rather than edited here directly. Has its own **Rework in chat** button: highlight a span of text and send it to the docked WB chat for a targeted rewrite, same as the Lore Sheet's own version but scoped to this field |
| **Type** | Specific type within category (e.g., "Protagonist", "Villain", "Capital City") |
| **Status** | Narrative status: active, inactive, or historical |
| **Disable this entry** | Disabled entries won't be matched in text or included in AI context |

### Step 6: Save Your Entry

Click **Create** (or **Update** on an existing entry) to save.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Create entries for all major characters, locations, and important story elements before you start writing. This ensures the AI has a good understanding of your story world from the beginning.

### The docked World-Building chat

Pick a template from **New Chat** in the WB chat panel to start a focused session — Character, Location, and others each get their own system-prompt framing. A **Playbook** rail icon offers **Guided setup** (Light/Standard/Grill-me style dropdown, seeds an opening line rather than running a scripted interview) for any template.

The **Character** template gets two extra, opt-in tools:

- **Psych module** — an **Add psych prompt** button seeds the composer with a structured character-psychology interview prompt (MBTI/Enneagram/motivation/speech patterns). This is a writing aid only, not Codex state — it never goes through the Codex approve/reject pipeline; results render read-only on the entry.
- **Playbook pack** — a **Use playbook pack** toggle (next to Guided setup) arms a starter question pack for the interview; **Open Playbooks** jumps to the dedicated Playbooks tool to browse/manage packs, reachable via Settings → Writing tools or this link.

## Managing Your Lorebook

The Lorebook provides powerful tools for organising, filtering, and managing your story elements.

### Filtering and Sorting

**Category Filters**
Use the tabs at the top of the Lorebook page to filter entries by category: All, Characters, Locations, Items, Events, Notes, Synopsis, Starting Scenario, Timeline.

**Search and Sort**
Use the search bar to find specific entries by name, description, or tags. Sort entries by Name, Category, Importance, or Created Date. Turn on "All categories" next to the search box to search every category at once — useful in a large lorebook when you don't remember which category an entry is in.

**Cards | List view**
A toggle at the top of the entry list switches between a card grid (thumbnail, full description preview) and a denser row layout (name, level badge, importance, up to 3 tag chips — no description) that fits more entries on screen at once. A category with 12 or more entries defaults to List automatically the first time you view it; your explicit choice is remembered after that.

### Organizing with Folders

Folders are a cosmetic filing layer for a single category's entries within one scope (a story or series) — they don't affect what the AI sees, only how the list is organised for you. They're independent per category, so Characters and Locations each have their own folder tree.

**Creating a folder:** Click the folder-plus icon at the top of the folder sidebar. Folders can be nested up to 3 levels deep — right-click a folder and choose "New subfolder" to nest one inside it.

**Filing entries:** Drag an entry's card or row from the main list onto a folder in the sidebar to file it there. Drag onto "Unfiled / All" to remove it from any folder.

**Reordering folders:** Hover a folder row to reveal a grip handle on its left, then drag it up or down to reorder it among its siblings (folders sharing the same parent). Dragging a folder onto one with a *different* parent doesn't move it there — that's what "Move to…" is for, below.

**Moving a folder:** Right-click a folder and choose "Move to…" to reparent it under a different folder (or to the root).

**Deleting a folder:** Right-click and choose "Delete." Nothing inside is deleted — subfolders and entries filed in it just move up to its parent (or become Unfiled if it was a root folder).

### Editing and Deleting Entries

**Editing:** Click the entry card (or its edit icon) to open the same full Lore Sheet editor described in the Creating Entries tab, pre-filled with current values. Make changes and click "Update" to save.

**Deleting:** Click the trash icon on the entry card and confirm the deletion.

> [!TIP]
> Any pending Codex change — whether from Sync, a chat proposal, or the "Suggest Codex updates" scan — shows a real diff against the entry's current state before you approve it: added/removed for list fields (Wardrobe, Wounds, Items), before → after for labeled fields (Appearance, custom fields).

> [!WARNING]
> Deletion is permanent and cannot be undone.

### Understanding Level Badges

Each lorebook entry displays a badge indicating its level:

| Badge | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Global** | Available everywhere |
| **Series** | Shared within series |
| **Story** | Story-specific only |

> [!TIP]
> When viewing a story's lorebook, you'll see entries from all accessible levels (global, series if applicable, and story). Each entry's badge shows its origin level.

### Enabling and Disabling Entries

Instead of deleting entries, you can disable them temporarily. Useful when you want to exclude certain entries from AI context without losing the information.

- Click the eye icon on an entry card to toggle enabled/disabled
- Disabled entries appear faded with a "Disabled" badge
- Use the "Show Disabled" toggle to view or hide disabled entries

### The Secrets tab

Click **Secrets** in the Lorebook toolbar to open a story-wide view of every character's Codex secrets at once — an alternative to checking each character individually. It lists every secret with its Hidden/Revealed state, an All/Hidden/Revealed filter, and a link to jump straight to the owning character. Click the eye icon on any row to reveal or re-hide it, or use the chapter dropdown to set/change its auto-reveal chapter, right from this tab.

An **Add secret** button lets you create a new one directly here too — pick a character and type the secret. It's a real shortcut, but if the secret connects to facts you're already establishing for that character (their appearance, wardrobe, backstory), it's usually better to add it from that character's own Codex instead, where you can see the rest of their state while you write it.

### Import & Export

**Exporting Entries:**
1. Navigate to the Lorebook page
2. Click the Export button in the toolbar
3. Select which entries to export (by level, category, or all)
4. A JSON file downloads with all entry data

**Importing Entries (JSON):**
1. Click the Import button
2. Select a JSON file exported from another story
3. Review entries to be imported
4. Confirm to add them to your lorebook

*Name collisions are handled with unique suffixes. Level assignments are preserved or can be reassigned during import.*

**Importing a reference document (PDF/DOCX/MD/TXT):**
The same Import button also accepts a PDF, DOCX, Markdown, or plain-text file — pick one of those instead of a `.json` file and the AI extracts a draft entry from it (name, description, and structured Codex/place fields where it can find them) instead of doing a literal JSON import. The draft opens as its own tab — "Imported draft — review before saving" — with a fully populated Lore Sheet (reverse-compiled the same way an existing entry would be), so you can edit anything before it becomes a real entry. Embedded images in the source document are extracted too (the largest one, by area, becomes the entry's image). This path always picks the single most central subject in the document, even if it describes more than one.

**Importing multiple entries from one document:**
Use the separate **Import multiple** button (next to Import) when your reference document describes *several* characters or locations at once — a "character bible" with a profile per person, for example. It extracts one draft per subject instead of picking just one, and shows them all in a checklist dialog with a checkbox per entry — uncheck anything you don't want, then **Create N entries** to save the rest for real. An entry that's really a faction/organization with its own described structure comes back as its own Note entry rather than being folded into a character's description.

> [!TIP]
> **Import/Export Use Cases:**
> - **Backup:** Export all entries before making major changes
> - **Reuse:** Export character/location entries to use in another story
> - **Collaboration:** Share lorebook entries with co-authors
> - **Templates:** Create template lorebook sets for recurring story types

### Best Practices

- Regularly review and update entries as your story evolves
- Use consistent naming conventions for related entries
- Keep descriptions concise but informative
- Use tags strategically to ensure proper matching
- Disable entries temporarily rather than deleting if unsure

## Integration with Writing

The true power of the Lorebook comes from how it integrates with your writing process and AI generation.

### Automatic Tag Matching

As you write, Story Labyrinth automatically scans your text for matches with your Lorebook entries' tags.

**How Tag Matching Works:**

1. Each Lorebook entry has tags (including its name)
   - *Example: A character "Harry Potter" might have tags: "Harry Potter", "The Boy Who Lived", "Gryffindor Seeker"*

2. When your text contains any of these tags, the system identifies a match
   - *Example: Writing "Harry walked into the room" matches the "Harry Potter" entry*

3. Matched entries are collected and viewable in the editor sidebar
   - *Click "Matched Tags" in the editor to see which entries match your current chapter*

### AI Context Integration

When you use AI generation features like the Editor Chat, matched Lorebook entries provide context to the AI.

**How AI Integration Works:**

1. When you generate or chat about a chapter, the system identifies relevant Lorebook entries
   - *Includes entries matched anywhere in your chapter's text*

2. The system searches across all accessible levels (global, series, story)
   - *Hierarchical matching ensures access to all relevant world-building*

3. Descriptions from these entries are included in the AI context
   - *The AI understands your characters, locations, and story facts*

4. The AI uses this context to generate accurate and consistent content
   - *It knows character personalities, location details, and important facts*

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The AI automatically uses context from all lorebook levels you have access to. Series-wide characters and world-building are always available alongside story-specific details, ensuring consistency across interconnected narratives.

### Viewing Matched Entries in the Editor

While writing, view which Lorebook entries are actively matched in your current chapter:

1. In the chapter editor, look for the "Matched Tags" button in the sidebar or toolbar
2. Click to open a drawer showing all matched lorebook entries
3. The drawer displays: entry name, category, triggering tags, level badge, description preview
4. Review matches to ensure the right context will be provided to the AI
5. If important entries are missing, mention them by exact name or tags in your text

> [!TIP]
> Lorebook matching updates as you type. As soon as you mention a character name or location tag, the corresponding entry becomes matched and available to the AI.

## Advanced Lorebook Strategies

**Strategic Tagging**
Create tags that appear naturally in your writing. Include variations, nicknames, and common references.

**Hierarchical Organisation**
Use importance levels (major, minor, background) to prioritise which information is most critical for the AI.

**Consistent Descriptions**
Write descriptions in a consistent style and format. Focus on information relevant for AI generation.

**Regular Maintenance**
As your story evolves, update entries to reflect character development, changing relationships, and new story elements.
